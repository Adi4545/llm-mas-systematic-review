# Referee Report: Reviewer C — Technical Validity & Quantitative Metrics Critic

**Target Manuscript:** `reviews/manuscript_v2_edited.md` (*When Do LLM Multi-Agent Systems Outperform Single Agents? A Systematic Review of Compute-Normalized Evidence, Coordination Topologies, Failure Mechanisms, and Evaluation Validity*)  
**Audited Data Artifacts:** `data/evidence_matrix.json`, `data/quality_appraisal.csv`, `agents/agent8_metrics_analysis.md`, and primary literature via open web verification.

## 1. Overall Technical Assessment & Recommendation

**Recommendation: Major Revision (Accept Contingent on Statistical Robustness Fixes, Tuple Pool Cleaning, and Technical/Citation Corrections)**

The manuscript addresses an urgent, consequential question in LLM multi-agent systems (`LLM-MAS`) evaluation: whether reported MAS accuracy gains survive compute-matched, prompt-matched single-agent (`SAS`) baselines. Its core methodological commitments—prohibiting cross-benchmark raw score pooling, introducing the four-tier SAS rigor stratification (`Tier 1`–`Tier 4`), auditing upfront optimization amortization (`C6`) and six confounder classes (`C1`–`C6`), separating parallel collectives from stateful interactive topologies, and segregating OpenReview rejections/withdrawals (`E8`)—are conceptually sound and well suited for *ACM Computing Surveys*.

Programmatic verification against `data/evidence_matrix.json` and `data/quality_appraisal.csv` confirms that the primary PRISMA totals (`44` studies, `32` archival, `3` workshops, `9` preprints), `QA1`–`QA7` scores (`mean = 77.4%`, `26` High, `18` Moderate, `0` Low), confounder prevalences, and core stratified tuple arithmetic (`49` tuples: `3/16 = 18.8%` Tier 1 wins vs. `25/33 = 75.8%` Tier 2+3 wins; `-57.0 pp`; archival `-50.1 pp`) reproduce cleanly from the underlying JSON extraction database.

**However, a rigorous experimental and statistical audit uncovers six Major Concerns (including two Blocking methodological/validity issues) and seven Minor Concerns that must be resolved before publication:**
1. **Unit-of-analysis inflation and invalid/nested tuples inside the 49-tuple denominator:** Equal weighting of `49` heterogeneous tuples across `23` studies introduces severe **study-clustering bias** (e.g., `SR001` supplies `2/3` of all Tier 1 wins; `SR018` supplies `6` tuples in Tier 2; `SR011` supplies `3` AlpacaEval tuples against the same `57.5` GPT-4o score; while `SR004`'s `980` settings count as `1` tuple). Worse, **at least 7 of the 49 tuples** are either **non-`MAS-vs.-SAS` comparisons** (`SR015` Tuples 2–3 compare an optimized graph vs. unoptimized graph and node-optimized $N=1$ ReAct vs. unoptimized ReAct; `SR007` Tuple 2 reports `Heter-MAD` vs. `Homogeneous MAD`), or **nested/double-counted composites** (`SR014` Tuple 5 is the 4-benchmark average of Tuples 1–4; `SR002` Tuples 1–2 are subsets of Tuple 3; `SR020` Tuple 2 overlaps Tuple 1). Crucially, our independent sensitivity test shows the `-57.0 pp` attenuation **holds strongly** under both deduplicated valid tuples (`-52.8 pp`: `23.1%` vs. `75.9%`) and fractional study-level clustering (`-61.9 pp`: `13.0%` vs. `74.9%`), so adding study-level robustness and removing invalid tuples strengthens rather than weakens the thesis.
2. **Cross-study causal over-attribution:** The manuscript repeatedly claims to connect `MAST`, `Who&When`, `VTFR`, `CORBA`, and `OANA` *"causally"* to `Tier 1` negative `MAS < SAS` outcomes across disjoint benchmarks, backbones, and frameworks (`L37`, `L46`, `L311`), and characterizes `CMAD` [12] (a doubly-TMLR-rejected workshop paper) as *"prov[ing]"* diversity collapse (`L314`).
3. **Specific denominator, citation, unit, and technical-definition errors:** An ad-hoc `9/10` denominator swap on `C6` (`L277`, artifact of `agent8_metrics_analysis.md` before `SR032` moved to `RQ4`), a swapped latency citation (`[26]` instead of `[18]` at `L279`), a swapped conformity citation (`[39]` instead of `[73]` at `L314`), an erroneous `P-ASR` acronym expansion (`L315`), an out-of-range error-amplification parenthetical (`4.4x ... (5.1x–17.2x range)` at `L293`/`L313`), percentage-point vs. relative-`%` mixing (`Self-MoA` `65.7%` vs. `59.1%`), an outdated `75` / `35/44` NV-slot count (`L480` vs. `208` scalar fields across `43/44` records), and missing Markov/Jensen rigor on `DPI` and `pass^k` (`L154`, `L156`).

### Major Concern 5 — Technical Precision Gaps in Section 7 (`pass^k`, `DPI`, `VTFR`) and Unexplained `16` vs. `20` Diagnostic Split
- **Severity:** **Significant**
- **Manuscript Location:** Section 7.1(3) (`L154`), Section 7.1(5) (`L156`), Section 8.1.1 (`L185`), Section 8.2 (`L245`).
- **Criticism & Supporting Evidence:**
  1. **`pass^k` Estimator and Jensen's Inequality (`L154`):** Defining `pass^k` simply as `"(\hat{p}^k under per-trial success probability \hat{p})"` is mathematically sloppy. In Yao et al. [66], for $n$ independent trials per task with $c$ successes, `pass^k` uses the unbiased hypergeometric estimator $\mathbb{E}_{\text{task}}[\binom{c}{k}/\binom{n}{k}]$ (not biased plug-in $(c/n)^k$). Moreover, across heterogeneous tasks with per-task success probabilities $\{p_i\}$, $\frac{1}{M}\sum_i p_i^k \ge \left(\frac{1}{M}\sum_i p_i\right)^k$ by Jensen's inequality; confounding task-level $p_i$ with benchmark-mean $\hat{p}$ underestimates `pass^k` when some tasks are deterministic (`p_i=1`) and others always fail (`p_i=0`).
  2. **Unqualified `DPI` Markov Hypothesis (`L156`):** Writing $I(X; \hat{Z}_{\text{MAS}}) \le I(X; Z_{\text{SAS}})$ omits the prerequisite **Markov chain** ($Y \leftrightarrow Z_{\text{upstream}} \leftrightarrow M_{\text{handoff}} \leftrightarrow \hat{Z}_{\text{downstream}}$ under a fixed input $X$ and no external side information). When agents access partitioned external evidence (`BrowseComp-Plus`, `Finance-Agent` [28]) or heterogeneous pretraining distributions (`Heter-MAD` [73]), the Markov chain breaks—which is essential to explain cleanly why `DPI` forbids coordination gains in closed-book reasoning (`SR002`) yet permits them in `Regime D`.
  3. **`VTFR` Conditioning (`L156`):** In Sheshanarayana & Goli [47], `VTFR` is conditioned specifically on **initially correct native judgments ($C_0$)** that fail or abstain under abstraction, rather than symmetric flips across all verifier judgments (including native errors flipping to correct).
