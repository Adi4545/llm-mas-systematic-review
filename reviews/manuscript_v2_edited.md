# When Do LLM Multi-Agent Systems Outperform Single Agents? A Systematic Review of Compute-Normalized Evidence, Coordination Topologies, Failure Mechanisms, and Evaluation Validity

---

## 2. Abstract

Large language model multi-agent systems (LLM-MAS) are widely promoted for reasoning, coding, and tool use, yet prior surveys rarely test whether gains survive equal-compute single-agent controls. This PRISMA review synthesizes 44 studies (2023–November 2026; 49 paired comparisons). MAS win 75.8% of unmatched or weak comparisons, falling to 18.8% wins and 50.0% losses under compute-matched parity (-57.0 percentage points; -50.1 in archival studies). We link negative returns to error amplification, conformity, handoff loss, unamortized search costs (9/10 optimization studies), absent `pass^k` reporting (0/24 comparative studies), and harness distortion, proposing a synthesis-derived four-axis taxonomy.

## 4. Introduction

Between 2023 and late 2026, Large Language Model-based Multi-Agent Systems (LLM-MAS)—encompassing peer debate, role-specialized software pipelines, centralized orchestrator-worker teams, and learned communication graphs—expanded substantially across reasoning, software engineering, and interactive tool-use benchmarks [17, 22, 44, 74, 85]. Early multi-agent prototypes frequently reported accuracy improvements over single-pass prompting [17, 55], prompting rapid adoption across both benchmark suites and enterprise workflows [35, 83]. However, controlled empirical audits conducted between 2024 and 2026 expose a persistent divergence between headline advocacy and compute-normalized performance. When total inference tokens, API expenditure, and prompt quality are held equal against strong single-agent systems (`SAS`)—such as few-shot chain-of-thought (`CoT`), multi-sample `Self-Consistency` (`SC`), or structured single-agent scaffolds—multi-agent architectures frequently match or underperform single-agent execution [28, 33, 39, 48, 51, 63, 73].

Simultaneously, large-scale execution trace audits demonstrate that current LLM-MAS pipelines fail in 41% to 86.7% of trajectories due to specification drift, inter-agent misalignment, and faulty verification [5]. Multi-round peer debate exhibits pervasive conformity and sycophancy collapse [9, 12, 27, 41, 60]. Measurement audits further complicate interpretation: single-run metrics conceal multi-trial reliability decay (`pass^k`) [45, 66], topology-optimization frameworks routinely omit upfront search expenditure from headline comparisons [63, 69, 71, 72, 74, 85], and benchmark harness defects introduce score distortions comparable to or exceeding typical multi-agent margins [47, 53, 59, 84].

Although at least 27 secondary surveys published or posted between 2024 and late 2026 catalog agent architectures, communication protocols, debate mechanisms, or failure-localization methods, none performs an integrated systematic meta-audit testing whether LLM-MAS outperform compute-matched `SAS` and explaining when gains vanish under experimental controls. Within *ACM Computing Surveys (CSUR)*, recent articles examine agent optimization [16], generalizability [76], security and privacy [23], game-playing agents [24], and classical multi-agent reinforcement learning (`MARL`) scaling [21], leaving empirical LLM-MAS versus `SAS` evaluation as a clear thematic gap. Broad reviews in *Artificial Intelligence Review* [1, 2, 10], *Journal of Artificial Intelligence Research* [42], *Frontiers of Computer Science* [56], and *Transactions on Machine Learning Research* [20, 37, 75] synthesize general agent capabilities, memory, or single-query routing without stratifying paired MAS-versus-SAS outcomes by compute parity. Specialized reviews of multi-agent debate [38, 50], communication and workflow graphs [64, 68, 83], efficiency engineering [32], coordination reliability [34], safety [13], trajectory attribution [54], and agent evaluation [36, 67] address valuable facets of the literature without resolving the core comparative question. This review makes four contributions:
1. **Four-Tier SAS Baseline Rigor Framework:** We classify all 44 included studies (including 24 comparative studies yielding 49 paired MAS-versus-SAS comparisons across 23 studies) into mutually exclusive baseline rigor tiers (`Tier 1` through `Tier 4`, plus `N/A-Diagnostic`), separating compute- and token-matched evaluations from unmatched or weak single-pass comparators.
2. **Quantification of Baseline-Rigor Attenuation and Publication-Status Sensitivity:** Without pooling raw scores across incompatible benchmarks, we measure a `-57.0` percentage-point (`pp`) drop in MAS win rates from unmatched or weak baselines (`75.8%`, `25/33`) to compute-matched `Tier 1` controls (`18.8%`, `3/16` wins versus `50.0%`, `8/16` outright losses), and confirm that this attenuation holds (`-50.1 pp`, `77.4%` to `27.3%`) when restricting solely to peer-reviewed archival articles.
3. **Causal Integration of Amortization, Failure Mechanisms, and Harness Validity:** We connect unamortized search compute (`9/10` optimization-bearing studies), omitted wall-clock latency (`81.8%` of corpus), `MAST` design failures (`41%–86.7%`), conditional error amplification (`17.2x` independent vs. `4.4x` centralized), conformity collapse (`11/12` dense-debate studies), Data Processing Inequality (`DPI`) handoff loss, absent comparative `pass^k` reporting (`0/24`), and harness defects into a unified explanation of multi-agent underperformance.

## 5. Review Objectives and Research Questions

Evaluating whether multi-agent orchestration outperforms single-agent execution requires isolating compute equivalence, communication topology, failure mechanisms, and measurement validity without assuming coordination is inherently beneficial. We structure this review around four objectives (`O1`–`O4`) mapped cleanly to four non-overlapping research questions (`RQ1`–`RQ4`):

- **Objective O1 (Compute-Normalized Effectiveness) → `RQ1`:** *Under what task structures, base-model capability regimes, and compute- or token-budget conditions do LLM-based multi-agent systems outperform, match, or underperform single-agent baselines?* `RQ1` tests whether MAS accuracy advantages survive parity in prompt demonstrations, total inference tokens (prompt, hidden reasoning, and completion), and sampling budgets (`Self-Consistency`, `Best-of-N`, or single-agent tool scaffolds), identifying task-dependency boundaries separating decomposable/parallelizable or context-overflowing tasks from sequential/state-coupled and bounded-window reasoning.
- **Objective O2 (Coordination Topologies, Scaling, and Amortization) → `RQ2`:** *How do different multi-agent coordination topologies and communication paradigms relate to effectiveness, inference token overhead, amortized optimization cost, and end-to-end latency?* `RQ2` distinguishes parallel voting ensembles from stateful interactive topologies (sequential chains, centralized stars, dense debate, adaptive sparse or learned directed acyclic graphs [`DAGs`], and selective escalation), auditing whether inference-time savings offset upfront search/training compute (`MCTS`, `RL`, `VGAE`, or prompt search) and critical-path latency.
- **Objective O3 (Mechanistic Failure Drivers) → `RQ3`:** *Which empirically documented mechanisms—including inter-agent error amplification, conformity or sycophancy cascades, handoff information loss, and specification or verification failures—account for multi-agent underperformance relative to single-agent execution?* `RQ3` traces negative `RQ1`/`RQ2` outcomes to structural, information-theoretic, and social-epistemic failure modes while isolating inter-agent coordination breakdowns from single-agent intra-loop bugs.
- **Objective O4 (Evaluation Validity and Confounders) → `RQ4`:** *To what extent do experimental confounders—including unmatched compute budgets, asymmetric prompting, evaluation-harness fragility, single-run variance masking (`pass^k` decay), and benchmark leakage—distort reported multi-agent comparisons across the primary literature?* `RQ4` evaluates corpus rigor (`QA1`–`QA7`), prevalence of six confounder classes (`C1`–`C6`), benchmark distortion bounds, and segregated peer-review rejection and withdrawal cases.

## 6. Review Methodology

### 6.1 Protocol Governance and Transparent Open-Web Retrieval Boundaries

This review combines the PRISMA 2020 reporting standard [40] with Kitchenham and Charters's software-engineering systematic review methodology [29], operationalized through within-study paired-delta synthesis and seven-criterion quality appraisal (`QA1`–`QA7`). All eligibility gates (`I1`–`I6`, `E1`–`E8`), comparator tier definitions, and the 19-field extraction schema (validated against JSON Schema Draft 2020-12) were frozen prior to search execution and revised solely through a documented post-screening reconciliation log.

Candidate searches were executed on **November 20, 2026** across open academic indexing and publisher metadata portals: `arXiv` (`cs.CL`, `cs.AI`, `cs.MA`, `cs.SE`, `cs.LG`), `OpenReview` (`TMLR`, `ICLR`, `NeurIPS`, `ICML`, `KDD` workshops), `ACL Anthology` (`ACL`, `EMNLP`, `Findings`, `SIGDIAL`, `AACL-IJCNLP`), `NeurIPS Proceedings`, `PMLR`, `ACM Digital Library` (reflecting *ACM CSUR*'s transition to 100% Open Access on January 1, 2026), `Nature Portfolio`, `SpringerLink`, `IEEE Xplore`, `Frontiers in Artificial Intelligence`, `Preprints.org`, and `Semantic Scholar`. **Retrieval Scope Boundary:** Our environment lacked subscription bulk API or CSV/BibTeX export access to **Scopus** and **Clarivate Web of Science (WoS)**, and non-open-access publisher PDFs behind paywalls (`IEEE Xplore`, `SpringerLink`, `Nature`) could not be scraped beyond public abstracts, metadata, or verified author-hosted `arXiv`/open-access mirrors. Rather than claiming exhaustive closed-database coverage, any paywalled record lacking an open mirror sufficient to verify methodology and outcomes was excluded (`E5`), and unexposed sub-cells inside admitted paywalled-mirror records were preserved verbatim as `"not verifiable from accessible open summary"`.

### 6.2 Search Strategy, Temporal Window, and Citation Snowballing

The primary inclusion window spanned **January 1, 2023 through November 20, 2026**, capturing the empirical wave of post-GPT-4 LLM-MAS research (retaining pre-2023 methodological and classical MAS works strictly for background). Six Boolean search strings (`Q1`–`Q6`) targeted: (`Q1`) SAS-vs.-MAS compute-matched comparisons; (`Q2`) coordination topologies, communication pruning, scaling, and amortized search costs; (`Q3`) error amplification, conformity, sycophancy, and trajectory failure attribution; (`Q4`) evaluation validity, `pass^k`, and harness fragility; (`Q5`) terminological boundaries and selective `SAS -> MAS` escalation versus single-query routers; and (`Q6`) venue-targeted `OpenReview`, `ACL Anthology`, `NeurIPS`, `PMLR`, and `ACM DL` retrieval capturing public rejection and withdrawal logs. Searches `S01`–`S09` executed `Q1`–`Q6` across open indexes, while `S10` and `S11` executed one-hop backward and forward snowballing anchored on primary benchmarks and prior surveys.

### 6.4 Eligibility Criteria, Status Hygiene, and Four-Tier Baseline Rigor Coding

Studies were admitted if they met all six inclusion criteria (`I1`–`I6`): (`I1`) English-language public availability between January 1, 2023 and November 20, 2026; (`I2`) empirical evaluation of an LLM-MAS ($N \ge 2$ LLM instances) along one of three pathways—(`I2a`) comparative `MAS`-vs.-`SAS` or multi-topology outcomes (`RQ1`/`RQ2`), (`I2b`) quantitative failure, conformity, or attribution analysis (`RQ3`), or (`I2c`) agentic evaluation-validity or reliability audit (`RQ4`); (`I3`) primary quantitative metrics; (`I4`) compliance with Section 7.2's terminological boundary; (`I5`) verifiable open full text or open summary record; and (`I6`) eligible publication status. Exclusion rules (`E1`–`E8`) filtered out-of-window or non-English manuscripts (`E1`), classical non-LLM swarm intelligence or weight-space Particle Swarm Optimization (`E2`) [19, 25], single-agent pipelines, prompt chains, or single-query model routers lacking MAS escalation (`E3`) [61, 82], non-empirical surveys and position papers (`E4`) [1, 2, 10, 13, 16, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24, 30, 32, 34, 36, 37, 38, 42, 50, 52, 54, 56, 64, 67, 68, 75, 76, 83], paywalled or unverified records (`E5`), deduplicated preprints superseded by archival proceedings (`E6`), ungrounded proxy metrics or single-agent security/niche suites (`E7`), and disqualified statuses (`E8`).

Every record was classified under an eight-category publication-status taxonomy: `Peer-reviewed journal` (`n = 3`), `Peer-reviewed conference` (`n = 29`)—together comprising **Tier A Archival (`32/44 = 72.7%`)**—`Workshop` (`n = 3`, **Tier B Peer-Reviewed**, raising total peer-reviewed representation to `35/44 = 79.5%`), `Preprint` (`n = 9`, **Tier C**), `Under review` (held out unless linked to a de-anonymized empirical preprint independently passing `QA`), `Withdrawn`, `Publicly rejected`, and `Retracted`. Author-initiated *withdrawals* were strictly separated from editorial *rejections*; `E8` records (`[3, 4, 31, 46, 65, 77]` alongside prior submission rounds `[11, 80]`) were barred from positive effectiveness claims and restricted to a segregated `RQ4` diagnostic audit.

To distinguish genuine multi-agent coordination benefits from hidden compute scaling, every comparative experiment was assigned to one mutually exclusive **SAS Baseline Rigor Tier**:
- **Tier 1 (Gold — Compute/Token-Matched Strong SAS):** Evaluates MAS against a competitive single-agent baseline (`Self-Consistency`, `Best-of-N`, few-shot `CoT`, or single-agent tool/KV-cache loop) on identical backbones and evaluation harness while matching total inference tokens, API spend, or sample budgets (`n = 9` studies, `16` paired tuples).
- **Tier 2 (Partial — Strong SAS, Unmatched Compute or Unamortized Search):** Retains a competitive SAS baseline on identical backbones and harness, yet allows unmatched multi-agent token budgets (`3x–15x`) or omits upfront search/training compute (`MCTS`, `RL`, `LoRA`) from parity (`n = 9` studies, `24` tuples).
- **Tier 3 (Weak — Single-Pass Zero-Shot SAS Only):** Benchmarks MAS exclusively against greedy $N=1$ zero-shot or single-pass `Vanilla`/`CoT` SAS without `Self-Consistency` or token parity (`n = 5` studies, `9` tuples).
- **Tier 4 (Invalid or Absent LLM SAS Comparator):** Omits any single-agent LLM comparator (`n = 1` study, Szlatincsán et al. [49] on embodied `PARTNR`, `0` paired tuples).
- **N/A-Diagnostic:** Non-comparative `RQ3` trace-attribution and `RQ4` benchmark-validity studies (`n = 20` studies, `0` MAS-vs.-SAS tuples; strictly including Kapoor et al. [26], which audits single-agent scaffolds such as `LATS` and `Reflexion` rather than $N \ge 2$ MAS).

### 6.5 Quality Appraisal (`QA1`–`QA7`), Anti-Pooling Rules, and ACM Generative AI Disclosure

Each admitted study was scored across seven 3-point criteria (`0 = Not Met`, `1 = Partially Met`, `2 = Fully Met`): `QA1` SAS Baseline Rigor (`N/A-Diagnostic` for non-comparative audits, setting `applicable_max_score = 12` instead of `14`), `QA2` Cost and Latency Accounting, `QA3` Amortization Transparency, `QA4` Multi-Run Stochastic Reliability, `QA5` Benchmark and Harness Integrity, `QA6` Architectural and Code/Prompt Reproducibility, and `QA7` Claims–Evidence Parity. Composite quality bands were defined as **High** ($\ge 78.0\%$), **Moderate** ($42.5\%\text{–}77.9\%$), and **Low** ($< 42.5\%$). Preprints were admitted only upon clearing `total_raw_score >= 6` and `QA7 >= 1` (`9/9` passed). Averaging raw accuracy scores or percentage token savings across heterogeneous benchmarks, models, or harnesses is prohibited; synthesis relies solely on within-study paired tuples ($\Delta_{\text{MAS-SAS}} = \text{Score}_{\text{MAS}} - \text{Score}_{\text{SAS}}$) paired with mandatory Peer-Reviewed Archival versus Preprint sensitivity splits.

**Generative AI Disclosure (ACM Policy on Authorship):** Per the ACM Policy on Authorship, generative AI systems cannot be credited as authors and assume zero scholarly responsibility. Within our review methodology, AI agents assisted under continuous human/Research Director supervision in formulating Boolean search queries (`Q1`–`Q6`), running blinded Stage 1 and Stage 2 screening votes (with every boundary dispute adjudicated by the Research Director), populating the 19-field extraction matrix, executing Python validation scripts (`jsonschema Draft 2020-12`, BibTeX reconciliation, and table consistency checks), and conducting five structured clarity and accuracy editing passes. All DOIs, page ranges, venue statuses, and empirical numbers were verified against primary open-access records (`arXiv`, `OpenReview`, `ACL Anthology`, `PMLR`, and publisher metadata) under a strict zero-imputation covenant (`"not reported"` or `"not verifiable from accessible open summary"`), and full responsibility for the accuracy and integrity of this survey rests with the human authors.

## 7. Technical Background and Key Concepts

### 7.1 Core Operational Definitions

To resolve terminological drift identified in recent peer-review critiques [30], we establish six standard definitions used throughout the review:

1. **Single-Agent System (`SAS`):** A single LLM execution locus—spanning zero-shot or few-shot `CoT`, multi-sample `Self-Consistency` / `Best-of-N` majority voting from one model distribution, `Self-Refine`, deterministic multi-step pipelines (`Agentless` [61]), or a single-agent `ReAct` (`Reasoning + Acting`) tool loop—operating without inter-agent role splitting or distinct interacting peer/subordinate LLM instances.
2. **LLM-Based Multi-Agent System (`LLM-MAS`):** A system comprising $N \ge 2$ logically distinct LLM instances differentiated by explicit role prompts, isolated context windows/states, specialized tool permissions, or heterogeneous model backbones. Within `LLM-MAS`, we distinguish:
   - **Interactive / Stateful LLM-MAS:** Sequential Standard Operating Procedure (`SOP`) chains, centralized orchestrator-worker hubs, dense all-to-all peer debate, adaptive sparse or learned `DAGs`, and selective escalation pipelines in which agents condition subsequent steps on peer messages or shared state.
   - **Parallel / Voting LLM Collectives (`Independent / Voting Ensemble`):** $N \ge 2$ parallel LLM instances producing independent candidate responses combined via majority voting, confidence polling, or feed-forward aggregator layers (`MoA` [55], `Self-MoA` [33], crowd polling [14]). Retaining this stratum separately prevents conflating multi-sample aggregation with interactive deliberation.
3. **Reliability Metrics (`pass@k` vs. `pass^k`):** Whereas `pass@k` captures the probability that *at least one* of $k$ independent trials solves a task, `pass^k` (formalized by Yao et al. [66]) measures the probability that *all* $k$ independent trials succeed consistently ($\hat{p}^k$ under per-trial success probability $\hat{p}$). High `pass@1` or `pass@k` frequently obscures steep `pass^k` collapse in stateful multi-turn execution.
4. **Inference Compute vs. Amortized Optimization Cost:** *Inference compute* covers all test-time prompt, hidden reasoning/thinking, and completion tokens, API call counts, dollar spend, and wall-clock makespan (bounded by critical-path serial depth $\sum_{d=1}^D t_d$ across sequential rounds versus $\max_i t_i$ across ideal parallel branches). *Amortized optimization cost* covers all upfront tokens, GPU hours/memory, and API expenditure incurred prior to deployment to search workflows via Monte Carlo Tree Search (`MCTS` [74]), train low-rank graph masks or variational graph autoencoders (`VGAE` [71, 72]), learn routing policies via reinforcement learning (`RL` [69, 85]), or fine-tune critics via Low-Rank Adaptation (`LoRA` [43]). Full lifecycle evaluation requires amortizing upfront cost over deployment query volume $Q$ relative to a break-even threshold $Q_{\text{break-even}}$.
5. **Information-Theoretic and Verification Transport Metrics:** Following Tran and Kiela [51], natural-language handoffs between upstream and downstream agents act as Markov transformations constrained by the Data Processing Inequality (`DPI`), $I(X; \hat{Z}_{\text{MAS}}) \le I(X; Z_{\text{SAS}})$, risking irrecoverable loss of latent intermediate reasoning state. Following Sheshanarayana and Goli [47], Verification Transport Failure Rate (`VTFR`) quantifies the proportion of verifier judgments that flip solely when an execution trace is abstracted, summarized, or compacted across a delegation or macro-tool boundary rather than evaluated verbatim.
6. **Conditional Empirical Association vs. Universal Law:** Quantitative thresholds reported in primary studies—such as Kim et al.'s `~45%` baseline SAS accuracy saturation point or `17.2x` versus `4.4x` error-amplification multipliers [28]—are treated strictly as *conditional empirical associations* bounded by tested model generations, prompts, and benchmarks, not as invariant universal laws.

### 7.2 Terminological Boundary: LLM-MAS vs. Classical Swarm Intelligence and Classical MAS

A frequent pitfall in recent LLM literature is the uncritical transfer of classical distributed-AI terminology onto stateless prompt chains or single-context workflows. La Malfa et al. [30] show that many systems labeled "multi-agent" lack genuine distributed autonomy, formal communication semantics, or social-choice guarantees, instead wrapping single-model prompting loops in anthropomorphic role descriptions. ### 8.1 RQ1: Compute-Normalized Effectiveness and Conditional Regimes

#### 8.1.1 Problem, Existing Claims, and the Baseline-Rigor Attenuation Signal

*Problem and Existing Claims:* Determining whether multi-agent collaboration genuinely improves task performance requires separating coordination value from raw inference-compute scaling. Early LLM-MAS proposals frequently benchmarked $N$-agent multi-round teams consuming `3x–15x` extra tokens against single-pass greedy prompting, reporting broad win claims across reasoning and coding suites [17, 55, 71, 72]. However, without equal-compute `Self-Consistency` or few-shot `CoT` controls, such comparisons confound architectural collaboration with simple test-time sampling or prompt enrichment.

*Empirical Evidence and Sensitivity Testing:* Across the 44 included studies, 24 carry a `Tier 1`–`Tier 4` SAS rigor tier (`9` Tier 1, `9` Tier 2, `5` Tier 3, `1` Tier 4 [49]), while 20 are `N/A-Diagnostic` (`11` `RQ3` + `9` `RQ4`, including Kapoor et al. #### 8.1.3 Cross-Study Comparison, Interpretation, and Limitations (`RQ1`)

*Cross-Study Comparison and Interpretation:* Contrasting the 3 `Tier 1` wins against the 10 `Tier 1` losses and ties reveals three coherent boundary moderators:
1. **Task Dependency Geometry:** Compute-matched MAS succeed when tasks decompose into parallel, decoupled sub-tasks—such as analyzing independent financial filings on *Finance-Agent* (`+80.8% / +80.9%` relative) or parallelizing retrieval on *BrowseComp-Plus* (`+9.2%` relative) [28], or modular *CommonGen-Hard* tree generation (`+15.53 pp`, though random graphs lost `-2.53 pp` [44]). Partitioning also provides an advantage when single-agent inputs suffer extreme noise or context rot (`SR002` [51]), or when non-zero-sum collaborative debaters surface complementary evidence to an explicit verifier (`ColMAD` [8]). Conversely, when every action mutates shared sequential state (*PlanCraft* `-39% to -70%` [28]), requires multi-hop synthesis within a unified context (`-3.9` to `-4.1 pp` [51]), or shares a finite token window $W$ where reading peer outputs consumes generation headroom (`93%` of `980` configs [39]), multi-agent splitting degrades accuracy.
2. **Conditional Capability Saturation, Prompt Parity, and KV-Cache Collapse:** Across `260` configurations spanning `6` benchmarks in *Nature Machine Intelligence* (expanded from `180` configurations across `4` tasks in their initial `v1` preprint), Kim et al. [28] identify a conditional empirical threshold ($p < 0.001$): once single-agent accuracy exceeds `~45%`, adding multi-agent coordination yields diminishing or negative returns because coordination overhead (`+58%` to `+285%`) exceeds the remaining resolvable error margin. Critically, this `~45%` point is a conditional empirical association bounded by tested 2025–2026 backbones and benchmarks rather than a universal cutoff. Likewise, Qineng Wang et al. [57] (`Tier 2`) show `MAD` outperforms `CoT` only when few-shot demonstrations are withheld from SAS (`C4`), vanishing once demonstrations are restored. For homogeneous workflows, Xu et al. [63] (`OneFlow`) compile MCTS-discovered `AFlow` pipelines into persona switches within one LLM context, matching accuracy within `<1%` while cutting latency and USD expenditure through `KV-cache` (`key–value cache`) prefix reuse.
3. **Heterogeneity, Verifiers, and Reconciling Apparent Conflicts:** Mixing weaker models into feed-forward voting (`MoA` [55], `Tier 3`) trails single-best-model `Self-MoA` (`-6.6%` `AlpacaEval 2.0`, `-3.8%` reasoning [33]) because weaker proposers lower candidate pool quality. Conversely, cross-family *interactive* debate (`Heter-MAD` [73], `+6.4%` to `+8.2%` over homogeneous `MAD`) disrupts shared single-model blind spots, though centralized orchestrator sensitivity varies across model families (`Claude` vs. `OpenAI`/`Gemini` [28]). Crucially, without an external verifier, scaling polling up to `25x` inference compute produces zero consistent truthfulness gain [14]. Across the full `RQ1` pool, apparent contradictions resolve cleanly under confounder controls: Du et al.'s [17] `+7.8 pp` MMLU claim relied on `~6x` unmatched calls against GPT-3.5 single passes (`C1`), which fails under `Tier 1` `SC` parity [39, 48, 73]; `MoA` [55] omitted `Self-MoA` [33]; `AFlow` [74] omitted upfront `MCTS` search relative to `OneFlow` [63]; `GPTSwarm` [85] trailed `CoT` in Qian et al.'s [44] replication (`0.2368` vs. `0.3544` MMLU; `0.4969` vs. `0.6098` HumanEval); and `CopMAD` vs. `ColMAD` [8] shows zero-sum persuasion incentives invert oversight returns (`-15% to -19%` vs. `+10 to +19 pp`).

*Limitations and Implications (`RQ1`):* Even within `Tier 1`, raw cell percentages in paywalled or summarized appendices (`[28, 48, 63, 73]`) remain `NV`, and `8/9` `Tier 1` studies omit `pass^k` consistency. Practically, teams should treat `Self-Consistency`, few-shot `CoT`, and KV-cached single-LLM execution as mandatory default baselines before introducing multi-agent coordination.

### 8.2 RQ2: Coordination Topologies, Amortization Blind Spots, and Latency

*Problem and Existing Claims:* Multi-agent frameworks frequently claim substantial efficiency gains by replacing dense all-to-all debate graphs with learned sparse DAGs, graph neural networks, or routing controllers (`[69, 71, 72, 74, 85]`), reporting token reductions of `28%` to `95%`. Yet test-time inference savings mean little if upfront optimization compute (`C6`), wall-clock serialization penalties, or weak dense-graph denominators conceal true deployment cost.

Zero included studies test a standalone `Sequential Chain / SOP` or `Centralized Star` in isolation; both appear solely within multi-topology grids (`[28, 44, 49, 51, 57, 63, 81]`) and `RQ3` trace audits. *Limitations and Implications (`RQ2`):* Because `9/10` optimizers omit $Q_{\text{break-even}}$ curves, production ROI remains unverified outside `OneFlow` KV-cache simulation [63] and online uncertainty escalation [6]. Future topology optimization papers must amortize upfront search compute over explicit deployment horizons and benchmark against equal-latency `Self-Consistency`.

### 8.3 RQ3: Mechanistic Explanations for Multi-Agent Degradation and Failure

*Problem and Existing Claims:* If unmatched compute inflates apparent MAS gains (`RQ1`) and unamortized topologies inflate efficiency claims (`RQ2`), what mechanisms explain why multi-agent systems frequently lose *even when given equal or greater inference tokens*? Early multi-agent literature attributed coordination failures mainly to weak base prompts or inadequate role descriptions, assuming more turns or refined personas would restore accuracy.

*Empirical Evidence:* Across all 44 studies (`F17`), documented failure causes cluster into seven empirical mechanisms: **Error Amplification (`26/44`, `59.1%`)**, **Termination/Verification Failure (`19/44`, `43.2%`)**, **Conformity/Sycophancy Collapse (`14/44`, `31.8%`)**, **Specification/Role Failure (`11/44`, `25.0%`)**, **Harness/Metric Artifact (`11/44`, `25.0%`)**, **Handoff Information Loss / `DPI` (`6/44`, `13.6%`)**, and **Prompt-Injection / Collusion / Blocking (`5/44`, `11.4%`)** (only `2/44` studies [44, 63] document none). 1. **Structural Specification and Misalignment Breakdown (`MAST`):** Cemri et al. [5] (`1,642+` traces across `7` frameworks; human $\kappa=0.88$, LLM-judge $\kappa=0.77$) document `41%–86.7%` failure rates spanning System Design (`~44.2%`: step repetition `15.7%`, termination unawareness `12.4%`, task disobedience `11.8%`, history loss `2.8%`, role disobedience `1.5%`), Inter-Agent Misalignment (`~32.35%`: reasoning-action mismatch `13.2%`, derailment `7.4%`, missed clarification `6.8%`, conversation reset `2.2%`, ignored input `1.9%`, withholding `0.85%`), and Verification Failures (`~23.5%`: wrong verification `9.1%`, incomplete verification `8.2%`, premature termination `6.2%`). Intervention tests show superficial prompt rewording leaves these failures intact without transactional state and decoupled verification.
2. **Topology-Modulated Error Amplification:** Across evaluated setups in Kim et al. [28], unvalidated `Independent` architectures amplify errors up to **`17.2x`** relative to SAS, whereas `Centralized` verification bottlenecks limit conditional amplification to **`4.4x`** (`5.1x–17.2x` range). When sequential state dependencies prevent verification between steps (*PlanCraft*), upstream hallucinations cascade into `-39% to -70%` net degradation.
3. **Conformity, Sycophancy, and Contractive Assimilation:** Across `Dense All-to-All Debate`, **`11/12` (`91.7%`)** studies report social-epistemic collapse (`[8, 9, 12, 17, 27, 39, 41, 43, 48, 60, 79]`). Controlled debates confirm statistically significant conformity driven by majority count and capability disparities [9, 60]. Dai [12] proves that endogenous consensus drives inter-agent correlation toward `1.0`, yielding **`18%–47%` miscoverage** under agreement-based stopping (`R^2 = 0.96`). Meanwhile, Chen et al. [8] separate persuasive cheap-talk hacking (`CopMAD`, `-15% to -19%`) from dissent suppression (`CosMAD`). Causal interventions—peer sycophancy priors (`+10.5 pp` [27]), anti-sycophancy prompt refinement [41], `ColMAD` incentives (`+10 to +19 pp` [8]), counterfactual `LoRA` critics (`+2.0 pp` [43]), and split-conformal certificates [12]—validate that social assimilation actively destroys correct minority reasoning unless mitigated.
4. **Information Loss (`DPI`), Delegation Opacity, and Blocking:** Natural-language handoffs lose mutual information under `DPI` bounds [51], peer reading compresses internal thinking headroom inside finite windows $W$ [39], and partial output-only logs strip causal attribution (`TraceElephant` `+76%` to `76.5%` gain upon full observability [7]). In embodied collaboration, speculative action prediction (`OANA`) induces `15/16` regressions versus grounded `KE` (`-25%` steps) [49], while `CORBA` [81] exploits benign recursive blocking loops (`~92%–100%` Paralyzing Attack Success Rate [`P-ASR`] within `1.60–1.90` Peak Blocking Turn Number [`PTN`]).
5. **Attribution Ceilings and Single-Loop Exclusion:** Zero-shot root-cause localization scored poorly on `Who&When` (`53.5%` agent / `14.2%` step [78]) and `TRAIL` (`11%` joint at `>200k–6M` tokens [15]), motivating `AgenTracer` (`+18.18%` [70]) and `AgentLocate` (`76.19%` [62]). Yet `TRIAGE` [58] demonstrates that in **`59.3%`** of `Who&When` traces, an LLM council identifies a valid repair step differing from the single-gold label—establishing causal non-uniqueness in cyclic multi-agent dialogues (`73.9%` Top-5 on `Who&When`; `93.2%` Top-10 on `TRAIL`). Crucially, we exclude Zhu et al.'s `AgentDebug` [82] (`E3`), which classifies `17` **intra-agent single-loop module errors** inside single `ReAct`/`CodeAct` trajectories rather than $N \ge 2$ inter-agent interactions.

*Limitations and Implications (`RQ3`):* Most mitigation interventions (`[12, 27, 41, 43]`) have yet to be jointly evaluated against `Tier 1` compute-matched `Self-Consistency` on hardened software sandboxes, underscoring the need to distinguish single-loop debugging from inter-agent coordination remedies.

### 9.3 Cross-Agent Topology Security, Liveness, and Engineering Trade-Offs

Recent security and safety surveys establish that agentic architectures expand single-model prompt-injection exposure into memory poisoning, tool misuse, and inter-agent privilege escalation (`[13, 23]`). Our synthesis isolates two topological vulnerabilities specific to $N \ge 2$ interactive networks. First, `CORBA` [81] proves that semantically benign blocking messages exploit recursive collaboration loops across `AutoGen` and `CAMEL`, achieving `~92%–100%` `P-ASR` within `1.60–1.90` turns while bypassing single-agent safety filters—converting open broadcast topologies into denial-of-collaboration amplifiers. Second, `VTFR` [47] establishes that delegating verification across summarized subagent reports flips evaluator judgments in `30.9%` of transitions, allowing specification drift (`MAST` [5]) or flawed patches (`PatchDiff` [59]) to clear orchestrator gates unless full execution provenance is preserved (`TraceElephant` [7]).

From an engineering standpoint, practitioners must weigh inference spend, upfront optimization amortization, and wall-clock makespan jointly. Naive multi-round debate inflates tokens $O(N \cdot R)$ or $O(N^2 \cdot R)$ while serializing latency across turns [48]. Learned sparse graphs (`[69, 71, 72, 74, 85]`) reduce deployed inference tokens relative to unpruned complete graphs, yet `9/10` optimization frameworks omit upfront `MCTS`, `RL`, or `VGAE` training expenditure (`C6`). Whenever agents share homogeneous weights, single-context KV-cache prefix reuse (`OneFlow` [63]) eliminates inter-agent network hops while maintaining equivalent accuracy, compressing both latency and USD spend.

## 10. Research Gaps and Future Research Directions

Bridging the gap between our Tier I empirical consensus and the unresolved Tier IV–V questions in Table 9 requires shifting evaluation norms away from unnormalized leaderboard deltas toward lifecycle reliability engineering. Five concrete research priorities follow directly from the evidence gaps documented in Section 8:

1. **Standardized Compute- and $Q_{\text{break-even}}$-Amortized Pareto Benchmarking:** Future workflow optimizers, adaptive routers, and debate protocols should evaluate against `Tier 1` strong `SAS` baselines (`Self-Consistency`, `Agentless` [61], `OneFlow` KV-cached execution [63]) across equal total token budgets, disclosing upfront `C6` search/training spend and deriving $Q_{\text{break-even}}$ deployment thresholds alongside wall-clock latency (`QA2`).
2. **Mandatory `pass^k` Reliability and Hardened Harness Protocols:** Comparative MAS studies should report multi-trial consistency (`pass^k` for $k \ge 5$) [45, 66], per-cell dispersion (`SD` or `95% CI`), and risk coverage alongside `pass@1`, running on hardened suites featuring differential testing (`PatchDiff` [59]), deterministic DB/REST state validation (`WebArena Verified` [18]), and adversarial exploit patches (`BenchJack` [53], `ABC` [84]).
3. **Lossless Provenance Protocols Against `DPI` and `VTFR` Decay:** Extending `TraceElephant` [7] and `VTFR` provenance anchors [47] to `>200k`-token long-horizon traces (`TRAIL` [15]) is needed so orchestrators can verify subagent claims without re-ingesting full transcripts or incurring `30.9%` transport error.
4. **Unified Incentive, Conformal, and Anti-Sycophancy Protocols:** Combining collaborative non-zero-sum incentives (`ColMAD` [8]), label-free peer sycophancy priors [27], and split-conformal coverage certificates (`CMAD` [12]) merits `Tier 1` evaluation on interactive software and web-navigation benchmarks to determine whether social-epistemic collapse can be prevented without sacrificing minority insights.
5. **Liveness-Preserving Topology Guards and Causal Attribution:** Designing runtime dialogue-entropy and recursion-depth caps against contagious `CORBA` blocking [81] without triggering false deadlocks that suppress legitimate clarification (`MAST` `FM-2.3` [5])—coupled with causal counterfactual graphs resolving `59.3%` single-step attribution ambiguity (`TRIAGE` [58])—remains an open systems problem.

## 11. Threats to Validity and Review Limitations

We evaluate limitations of this systematic review across four standard methodological dimensions [29]:

- **Construct Validity:** Primary papers apply "multi-agent" and "single-agent" inconsistently, frequently conflating stateless parallel voting with interactive deliberation [30] or substituting disagreement proxies for correctness [3]. We mitigated construct drift via Section 7.2's terminological boundary, separating parallel collectives (`[14, 33, 55]`) from stateful interactive MAS, coding comparators via `Tier 1`–`Tier 4`, and excluding single-loop debuggers (`AgentDebug` [82]) and non-LLM swarms (`[19, 25]`). Where open summaries or paywalled appendices omitted raw sub-cell percentages, exact agent counts $N$, or runtime seconds (`75` full-field slots across `35/44` records plus sub-cell gaps), fields were preserved strictly as `"not verifiable from accessible open summary"` without imputation.
- **Internal Validity:** Screening and quality scoring carry inherent subjectivity. We enforced blinded dual screening (`Stage 1` $\kappa = 0.9489$, `Stage 2` $\kappa = 0.7699$, corpus binary $\kappa = 0.8575$) with formal Director arbitration, JSON Schema Draft 2020-12 validation, and primary-source verification of all identifiers (catching four swapped or malformed IDs and upgrading two preprints to verified NeurIPS 2025 publications).
- **External Validity:** Our synthesis spans 2023–November 2026 model generations and benchmarks; conditional empirical associations such as Kim et al.'s `~45%` capability cutoff [28] may shift as future test-time-scaling architectures alter baseline error distributions. Crucially, the absence of **Scopus** and **Clarivate Web of Science** bulk export access and exclusion of two paywalled IEEE Xplore proceedings (`E5`) concentrate coverage in open ML, NLP, and SE portals (`arXiv`, `OpenReview`, `ACL Anthology`, `PMLR`, `ACM DL`), potentially under-representing closed industrial-engineering venues.
- **Conclusion Validity:** Pooling raw accuracies across heterogeneous models and harnesses induces Simpson's paradox. We prohibited cross-benchmark numerical averaging, grounded all claims in within-study paired tuples ($\Delta_{\text{MAS-SAS}}$), ran three-tier publication-status sensitivity tests (`Peer-Reviewed Archival` vs. `Archival+Workshop` vs. `+Preprints`), and segregated `E8` rejections/withdrawals from positive claims.

## 12. Conclusion

Across 44 systematically screened empirical and diagnostic studies (2023–November 2026) and 49 within-study paired comparisons, the empirical evidence refutes generalized claims that LLM multi-agent systems reliably outperform single-agent execution. When inference compute, token budgets, and prompt demonstrations are matched against strong single-agent controls (`Tier 1`), MAS win rates decline by `-57.0 percentage points`—falling from `75.8%` (`25/33`) in unmatched or weak baselines to `18.8%` (`3/16`) wins versus `50.0%` (`8/16`) outright losses—and exhibit an equivalent `-50.1 pp` collapse (`77.4%` to `27.3%`) when all non-refereed preprints are excluded. Compute-matched multi-agent gains arise conditionally where tasks decompose cleanly into parallel retrieval or analysis branches, break severe single-context rot, or surface diverse evidence under cross-family debate paired with an external verifier. Outside these boundaries, error amplification, conformity and sycophancy collapse, `DPI` handoff loss, unamortized search compute (`9/10` optimization frameworks), absent `pass^k` testing (`0/24` comparative studies), and porous evaluation harnesses routinely erase multi-agent advantages. Enforcing `Tier 1` single-agent parity, full lifecycle amortization, hardened evaluation suites, and provenance-preserving coordination establishes a rigorous foundation for future multi-agent systems engineering.
