## Overall Novelty Assessment & Recommendation

**Recommendation: Major Revision** *(borderline **Reject** at ACM Computing Surveys in its current form; salvageable for TMLR only after resolving all Blocking claims–evidence parity, survey-overlap, recall, and taxonomy issues).*

The manuscript identifies a genuinely worthwhile empirical question—whether LLM multi-agent systems (`LLM-MAS`) outperform compute- and token-matched single-agent systems (`SAS`)—and its central quantitative observation (`-57.0 pp` drop in MAS win rates from `75.8%` in unmatched/weak `Tier 2+3` comparisons to `18.8%` wins and `50.0%` outright losses in `Tier 1` parity controls, preserved at `-50.1 pp` when restricting to peer-reviewed archival works) offers real analytical value beyond purely architectural catalogs (*Zhu et al.*, *Tran et al.*, *Motger et al.*, *Ala'anzy et al.*).

However, as submitted, the manuscript **cannot be accepted at *ACM CSUR* or certified as a *TMLR* survey** due to five converging novelty and differentiation failures:
1. **Substantial understatement of overlap** with *Li et al.* (Sept 2026, `10.20944/preprints202609.1639.v1`)—which *already* defines "The Collaboration Boundary" (when MAS vs. SAS is justified), matched baselines, and lifecycle/amortization cost accounting—and unacknowledged conflation/overlap with *Wang et al.* (*IEEE TSE* 2026) / *Anonymous* (*ACL ARR 2026 #2880/#3533*), *Motger et al.* (`arXiv:2607.26212`), and *Yue et al.* (`2026.findings-emnlp.1393`).
2. **Severe bibliographic and saturation blind spots**, including the silent deletion of a peer-reviewed *USENIX Security 2026* SoK (*Kim et al.*, `arXiv:2603.11088`) triggered by a literal title-substring cliché filter (`"Landscape of"`), omission of key already-cited surveys from Table 1 (*Moslem & Kelleher* TMLR, *Dantas et al.*, *Mallampati & Nagoriya*, *Yehudai et al.* ACL 2026, *Mohammadi et al.* KDD 2025), and complete omission of published 2025–2026 *ACM CSUR* (*Deng et al.*, July 2025, `10.1145/3716628`) and *TMLR* (*Wei et al.*, Sept 2026, `arXiv:2601.12538`) surveys alongside major TMLR Survey-Certified articles.
3. **Thin `Tier 1` comparative core (`n = 9` studies; `6` archival) paired with missed landmark MAS-vs.-SAS primary studies** (*Debate or Vote* NeurIPS 2025 Spotlight, *More Agents Is All You Need* TMLR, *Why Not Both?* `2505.18286`, *The Cost of Consensus* `2605.00914`, `2602.04234`, `2609.19759`) that directly govern the manuscript's claimed insights.
4. **Weak originality and internal contradictions in Section 9**, where the synthesis-derived `T × B × S × V` taxonomy mixes study-quality rubric tiers (`B1–B4`) with system/task categories without populating the 4D matrix, and the `Regimes A–D` workflow restates primary findings while contradicting itself on `>~45%` baseline SAS routing and `Q <= Q_break-even` escalation.
5. **TMLR/CSUR encyclopedic-listing and scope-dilution triggers** (`agent4_openreview_analysis.md` Cases T1 & T2): flat 1-study-per-row descriptive cataloging across Tables 5, 6, and 8 (`50` rows), sequential study-by-study prose walks in Sections 8.3 and 8.4.2 over single-agent benchmark/fault-localization papers (`20/44` non-comparative studies), and three colliding `"Tier"` scales (`Tier 1–4`, `Tier A–C`, `Tier I–V`).
