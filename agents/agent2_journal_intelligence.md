## Executive Summary

Across the seven evaluated AI, machine learning, and multi-agent systems journals, **ACM Computing Surveys (CSUR)**, **Artificial Intelligence Review (AIRE)**, and **Transactions on Machine Learning Research (TMLR)** emerge as the **Top 3 target venues** for an authoritative systematic review on Autonomous AI Agents, LLM-Based Agents, Multi-Agent Systems (MAS), Agentic AI, and AI Agent Swarms:

1. **Primary Target — ACM Computing Surveys (CSUR):** Highest prestige and visibility in computer science surveys (secondary/registry trackers report Clarivate JIF ~30.4 and CiteScore ~65.2, #1 Q1 in *CS Theory & Methods*); transitioned to **100% Open Access on January 1, 2026** ($\$0$ under institutional ACM Open agreements, or subsidized transitional rates of $\$250$ for ACM/SIG members / $\$350$ for non-members); actively publishes specialized 2025–2026 LLM-agent and MARL surveys (optimization, generalizability, security/privacy, game agents, scalable MARL) while **lacking a dedicated CSUR systematic review integrating LLM-based MAS, agentic orchestration, and agent swarms**. Requires strict adherence to a concise unstructured abstract (reported as $\le 100$ words in CSUR guideline summaries) and an original, non-chronological taxonomy/comparative methodology.
2. **Backup Target 1 — Artificial Intelligence Review (AIRE, Springer Nature):** Dedicated Q1 AI survey venue that explicitly embraces **PRISMA 2020** systematic reviews, explicit research questions, database search logs, and multi-dimensional evaluation matrices. Offers $\$0$ subscription publishing (or hybrid Gold OA). **Key strategic caveat:** AIRE has recently published three closely adjacent 2025–2026 reviews—notably *Ala'anzy et al. (Oct 2026)*, *Chowa et al. (Jan 2026)*, and *Abou Ali et al. (Nov 2025/Jan 2026)*—requiring a pre-submission editorial inquiry and unmistakable differentiation (e.g., swarm-scale multi-agent coordination, protocol formalisms, failure modes, and post-2025 multi-agent benchmarks).
3. **Backup Target 2 — Transactions on Machine Learning Research (TMLR):** **Diamond Open Access ($\$0$ APC)**, continuous rolling OpenReview peer review, no strict page/abstract word cap, and an official **Survey Certification** (plus *Featured Certification*) badge already awarded to five landmark LLM/agentic surveys (2023–2026). Evaluates soundness and clarity rather than subjective novelty, though reviewers expect deep ML/algorithmic/formal synthesis, strict double-blind anonymization, unmodified `tmlr.sty` LaTeX formatting, and an explicit first-page LLM-assistance footnote.

The remaining four journals are less suitable as primary targets for a broad, domain-general systematic review at this time: **JAIR** announced a **temporary freeze on all new Survey Article submissions starting December 1, 2026 through January 2027** to overhaul survey criteria; **Information Fusion** requires explicit multi-sensor/multi-source fusion framing, encourages pre-submission review proposals, and carries a $\$4,740\text{ USD}$ Gold OA APC; **JAAMAS** (while the flagship IFAAMAS MAS venue and host of the *When Foundation Models Meet Multi-Agent Systems* special issue) predominantly publishes original technical MAS research with rare surveys; and **EAAI** enforces strict desk-rejection rules requiring a concrete **real-world engineering application** alongside the AI contribution in the abstract.

1. **Aims and Scope:** Published under the JMLR ecosystem (`jmlr.org/tmlr`) on OpenReview, TMLR publishes original research advancing the computational and mathematical principles of intelligence through learning (in brains or machines), including new algorithms, insights into learning in intelligent systems, application studies exposing strengths/weaknesses, new tasks/frameworks, reproducibility studies, and analytical/visualization tools (`jmlr.org`). It explicitly prohibits expanded versions of prior published/parallel conference papers, though arXiv preprints and non-archival workshop papers are permitted.
2. **Guide for Authors:** Hosted via `jmlr.org/tmlr` and OpenReview. Uses **double-blind peer review** (manuscripts must be anonymized; all authors must maintain linked OpenReview profiles with conflict-of-interest histories). Requires a **Broader Impact Statement** whenever foreseeable societal harms exist, detailing risks and mitigations. Supports interactive HTML submissions (*TMLR Beyond PDF*).
3. **Review/Survey Paper Eligibility:** **Explicitly eligible without prior editor inquiry.** Unsolicited surveys undergo standard double-blind review; accepted surveys may additionally be awarded TMLR's official **Survey Certification**—defined on `jmlr.org` as recognizing papers that *"not only meet the criteria for acceptance but also provide an exceptionally thorough or insightful survey of the topic or approach."* (Other certifications include Reproducibility, J2C, Expert, Featured, and Outstanding.)
