## Executive Summary

A live saturation audit of **42 verified surveys, systematic reviews, and foundational position papers (2023–November 2026)** across **T1–T9** plus a newly synthesized **T10** demonstrates that most generic or mid-level LLM-MAS subtopics—evaluation/harness fragility (T2), broad multi-agent debate & failure attribution (T3), communication topology & scaling (T4), prompt injection & security (T5), classical-vs.-LLM swarms (T8), and coordination termination/verification (T9)—are **already occupied or heavily crowded** by 2025–2026 surveys, while uncertainty-aware escalation (T6) and MAS-specific `pass^k` reproducibility (T7) suffer from **sparse MAS-specific primary corpora**.

Two sharply bounded topics survive rigorous saturation, evidence, and venue-fit scrutiny:

1. **Primary Recommendation — T1 (Score: 93/100):** *Compute- and Token-Matched Empirical Comparisons of LLM Multi-Agent vs. Single-Agent Systems: When Multi-Agent Coordination Helps, When It Hurts, and What Confounders Distort Reported Gains.*  
   - **Why It Wins:** Zero published peer-reviewed survey in CSUR, TMLR, AIRE, JAIR, or JAAMAS—and no arXiv systematic review—systematically meta-audits primary head-to-head MAS vs. single-agent system (SAS) empirical evidence under compute/token-budget normalization. Its closest neighbor, **Li et al. (Sept 2026, Preprints.org)**, is an unrefereed engineering-optimization survey (*how* to compress topologies, runtime state, and offline workflows) rather than an empirical validity and confounder audit (*whether and when* MAS outperforms compute-matched SAS). Directly fills CSUR's verified thematic white space and aligns with TMLR Criterion 1 (*claims–evidence parity*).
2. **Fallback Recommendation — T10 (Score: 83/100):** *Inter-Agent Conformity Bias, Sycophancy Cascades, and Consensus Collapse in LLM Multi-Agent Systems.*  
   - **Why It Is Orthogonal & Viable:** Carved cleanly out of T3 by stripping away trace-level failure attribution (preempted by an anonymous 2026 ACL ARR survey) and broad Multi-Agent Debate (MAD) strategy cataloging (preempted by *Motger et al., July 2026*, `arXiv:2607.26212`), T10 isolates the un-surveyed social/epistemic breakdown mechanisms verified across ~18+ 2024–2026 primary studies (`arXiv:2509.23055`, `arXiv:2604.02668`, `arXiv:2506.01332`, `arXiv:2601.05606`, `arXiv:2606.00820`, `arXiv:2510.07517`, `arXiv:2605.27288`, *CONSENSAGENT* ACL Findings 2025).

## 3. Candidate-by-Candidate Saturation & Evidence Audit (T1–T10)

### T1: Compute- and Token-Matched Empirical Comparisons of LLM Multi-Agent vs. Single-Agent Systems
* **Exact Scope:** Systematic empirical meta-audit of peer-reviewed and benchmarked studies comparing LLM-based Multi-Agent Systems (debate, hierarchical orchestrator-worker, role-played pipelines, decentralized meshes) against **Single-Agent Systems (SAS)** (single-model CoT, Self-Consistency / Best-of-$N$, tool-equipped ReAct/reflexion loops), explicitly stratified by **compute/token normalization**, **task structure**, **base-model capability**, and **experimental confounders**.
* **Verified Anchor Evidence Corpus (Representative Sample Verified via `search_web`):**
  1. **Kim et al. (Google/MIT, Dec 2025, `arXiv:2512.08296`, *Towards a Science of Scaling Agent Systems*):** Evaluates 260 configurations across 6 benchmarks, 5 architectures, and 3 LLM families; shows MAS gains up to `+80.8%` on parallelizable financial reasoning vs. `-39%` to `-70%` degradation on sequential/tool-heavy tasks, capability saturation once single agents pass threshold performance, and up to `17x` error amplification in independent/decentralized topologies.
  2. **Tran & Kiela (Stanford, April 2026, `arXiv:2604.02460`):** Proves under equal thinking-token budgets that SAS matches or outperforms MAS on multi-hop reasoning; formalizes inter-agent text handoffs via the **Data Processing Inequality (DPI)** and uncovers API thinking-budget leakage artifacts (e.g., Gemini 2.5).
  3. **Zhao, Chen, & Su (Feb 2026, `arXiv:2602.04234`, *When Does Multi-Agent Collaboration Help? An Entropy Perspective*):** SAS outperforms MAS in `~43.3%` of evaluated settings; links failure to peak token/agent entropy.
  4. **Yuan et al. (Sept 2026, `arXiv:2609.19759`, *Rethinking Multi-Agent Collaboration: When More Is Less*):** Isolates task-dependency structure—MAS wins on long-horizon sparse-dependency tasks, SAS wins on tightly coupled sequential workflows.
  5. **Gao et al. (UIUC, May 2025, `arXiv:2505.18286`, *Single-agent or Multi-agent Systems? Why Not Both?*):** Shows frontier reasoning/long-context LLMs shrink the MAS margin and proposes SAS $\to$ MAS request cascading (~20% cost savings).
  6. **Choi, Zhu, & Li (NeurIPS 2025 Spotlight, `arXiv:2508.17536`, *Debate or Vote*):** Demonstrates simple independent Majority Voting accounts for most apparent multi-round MAD gains; models round-to-round debate as a martingale.
  7. **Li et al. (TMLR, `arXiv:2402.05120`, *More Agents Is All You Need*):** Shows test-time sampling/voting (*Agent Forest*) scales accuracy without inter-agent communication.
  8. **Zhang et al. (Feb 2025, `arXiv:2502.08788`, *Stop Overvaluing Multi-Agent Debate*):** Exposes weak baseline comparisons against compute-matched CoT/Self-Consistency and motivates model heterogeneity (*Heter-MAD*).
  9. **Smit et al. (ICML/arXiv, `arXiv:2311.17371`, *Should we be going MAD?*):** Finds out-of-the-box MAD fails to reliably beat Self-Consistency and exhibits extreme hyperparameter sensitivity.
  10. **Wang et al. (ACL 2024, *Rethinking the Bounds of LLM Reasoning*):** Shows a single agent with strong prompt demonstrations matches multi-agent discussion.
  11. **Cemri et al. (NeurIPS 2025 Datasets & Benchmarks, `arXiv:2503.13657`, *MAST*):** Audits 1,600+ traces across 7 MAS frameworks (`41%–86.7%` failure rates, $\kappa=0.88$), noting MAS frequently barely beats single-agent / best-of-$N$ baselines.
  12. **Sander et al. (July 2026, `arXiv:2607.27942`), Li et al. (June 2026, `arXiv:2606.00655`), Su & Wu (Feb 2026, `arXiv:2602.08272`), Shen et al. (March 2026, `arXiv:2603.29632`), Wu et al. (`arXiv:2511.07784`), Estornell & Liu (NeurIPS 2024), Anthropic (June 2025, *15x vs. 4x token multiplier; +90.2% on breadth-first open-ended search*):** Provide rich quantitative budget, topology, sample-efficiency, and production cost-benefit data, complemented by OpenReview baseline-audit failures (`AgenticNet` TMLR 7332, `SCALE` ICLR 2025 Sub 6097, `CoELA` ICLR 2024).
* **Critical Boundary vs. Closest Survey (*Li et al., Sept 2026, `preprints202609.1639`*):** Li et al. is an *engineering acceleration survey* classifying *how* to reduce MAS latency/tokens (Topology pruning, Runtime context compression, Offline workflow search, and Collaboration-Boundary routers). It does **not** conduct a systematic comparative meta-audit of *all SAS-vs.-MAS empirical outcomes*, nor does it construct a formal **Experimental Confounder Taxonomy** explaining why prior MAS literature systematically overstated gains (e.g., unmatched compute, voting illusions, prompt/harness asymmetry, exact-match drift).
* **Comparability Safeguard:** Because primary papers evaluate across heterogeneous benchmarks (`MATH500`, `GSM8K`, `SWE-bench`, `GAIA`, `BrowseComp`, `HumanEval`, `HotpotQA`), pooling raw accuracy deltas across tasks is methodologically invalid. T1 will synthesize via **matched-vs.-unmatched regime stratification**, **directional effect coding** (`MAS > SAS`, `MAS ≈ SAS`, `MAS < SAS` under token parity vs. unmatched compute), and **per-benchmark case syntheses**.

## 5. Final Topic Recommendations & Rejection Summary

### 5.1 Primary Recommended Topic: **T1**

* **Draft Working Title:**  
  *When Do LLM Multi-Agent Systems Outperform Single Agents? A Systematic Review of Compute-Normalized Evidence, Architectural Boundaries, and Evaluation Confounders*
* **Research Problem (One Paragraph):**  
  Between 2023 and 2026, LLM-based multi-agent systems (MAS) proliferated across software engineering, scientific discovery, and tool-use benchmarks, yet controlled empirical evaluations increasingly reveal that multi-agent pipelines frequently tie or underperform strong single-agent baselines (`SAS`) once total inference tokens, API cost, and latency are held constant. Existing surveys map MAS collaboration mechanisms, orchestration frameworks, or runtime efficiency optimizations, but none systematically meta-audits the comparative empirical record between MAS and SAS across compute-matched versus unmatched regimes. Without disentangling genuine architectural gains (e.g., parallel sub-domain decomposition, independent context isolation, heterogeneous verification) from experimental confounders (e.g., unnormalized test-time compute, majority-voting substitution for deliberation, weak single-agent prompts, exact-match/harness asymmetry, and error-amplification cascades), researchers and practitioners lack principled criteria for deciding when multi-agent coordination is warranted over a single capable agent.
* **Strict Scope Boundaries (PRISMA Inclusion / Exclusion):**
  * **Included:** Peer-reviewed and major preprint primary empirical studies (2023–Nov 2026) that either (a) directly benchmark an LLM-MAS (debate, hierarchical orchestrator-worker, role-play pipeline, or decentralized team/swarm) against an LLM single-agent baseline (`CoT`, `Self-Consistency`/`Best-of-N`, `ReAct`/single-agent tool loop), OR (b) establish controlled scaling/failure diagnostics of MAS-vs.-SAS regimes (`2512.08296`, `2604.02460`, `2602.04234`, `2609.19759`, `2508.17536`, `2503.13657`, etc.), coded explicitly for whether token/compute/cost matching and strong SAS baselines were implemented.
  * **Excluded:** Pure single-agent prompting/RAG/RL optimization papers without a multi-agent comparator; prompt-injection security and privacy surveys (covered by *He et al., CSUR 2026*; *Kim et al., USENIX Security 2026*); pure multi-agent memory maintenance (*Wang et al., 2026*); classical non-LLM MARL (*Geng et al., CSUR 2026*); standalone agent-harness engineering surveys (*Meng et al., 2026*; *Guo et al., 2026*); and multi-agent debate papers that neither compare against SAS/voting baselines nor control inference budgets (*Motger et al., 2026*).
* **Proposed Original Taxonomy (CSUR & TMLR Alignment):**  
  1. **Comparison Regime Axis:** Unmatched-Compute Claims vs. Token/Cost-Matched Controls vs. Sampling/Voting-Matched Controls.  
  2. **Task-Structure Boundary Axis:** Parallelizable / Sparse-Dependency / Context-Overflowing Tasks vs. Sequential / Tightly Coupled / Single-Context Tasks.  
  3. **Confounder Taxonomy (6 Classes):** Compute/Thinking-Token Inflation; API Budget Leakage; Sampling/Voting Masquerading as Deliberation; Asymmetric Single-Agent Baseline Under-Prompting; Harness/Exact-Match Sensitivity; Model-Capability Saturation Bias.  
  4. **Failure & Amplification Mechanisms Explaining Negative Returns:** Data-Processing-Inequality Handoff Loss; Inter-Agent Error Amplification; Specification/Verification Drift (*MAST*); Conformity/Diversity Collapse.
* **Explicit Comparison to Closest Prior Surveys:**
  * *vs. Li et al. (Sept 2026, Preprints.org `202609.1639`)*: Li et al. catalogs **how to compress/accelerate MAS** (Topology, Runtime, Optimization + Collaboration-Boundary routers); T1 meta-audits **empirical validity and SAS-vs.-MAS comparative outcomes**, coding confounders across matched vs. unmatched studies.
  * *vs. Motger et al. (July 2026, `arXiv:2607.26212`)*: Motger et al. taxonomizes 141 **Multi-Agent Debate** designs; T1 spans *all* MAS coordination paradigms (tool-use, coding/SWE, deep research, hierarchical teams, debate) strictly through the SAS-comparison and compute-normalization lens.
  * *vs. Zhu et al. (2026, Future Internet) & Tran et al. (Jan 2025, `2501.06322`)*: Those surveys map collaboration topologies, frameworks (LangGraph/CrewAI/AutoGen), and MCP/A2A protocols; T1 evaluates *empirical ROI and methodological validity* relative to single-agent baselines.
  * *vs. Ala'anzy et al. (AIRE Oct 2026) & CSUR 2026 Portfolio (`Du`, `Zhang`, `Geng`, `He`, `Hu`)*: None conducts a compute-matched SAS-vs.-MAS evidence audit or confounder synthesis.

### 5.2 Fallback Topic: **T10**

* **Draft Working Title:**  
  *When Multi-Agent Deliberation Collapses: A Systematic Review of Inter-Agent Conformity Bias, Sycophancy Cascades, and Consensus Pathology in LLM Systems*
* **Research Problem (One Paragraph):**  
  Multi-agent LLM architectures are widely deployed under the assumption that cross-agent critique and consensus formation correct individual reasoning errors, yet empirical studies from 2024–2026 demonstrate that instruction-tuned LLMs routinely succumb to peer conformity, inter-agent sycophancy, and identity-dependent favoritism—collapsing prematurely onto wrong-but-confident majorities even when agents privately retain correct solutions. While recent surveys catalog multi-agent debate protocols (*Motger et al., 2026*) or trace-level failure localization (*ACL ARR 2026*), no systematic review synthesizes the social-epistemic dynamics driving consensus collapse across multi-agent architectures. Synthesizing this corpus around normative vs. informational conformity, inter-agent sycophancy metrics, topological and identity-skew amplifiers, and structural mitigations (*CONSENSAGENT*, response anonymization, peer-sycophancy priors, heterogeneity) establishes a rigorous taxonomy of epistemic failure modes for collaborative AI.
* **Strict Scope & Differentiation:** Focuses strictly on peer-to-peer conformity, sycophancy propagation, opinion-herding/echo chambers, diversity collapse, and anti-conformity interventions in LLM-MAS; explicitly excludes trace-localization 5Ws+H debugging (*ACL ARR 2026*), generic MAD protocol catalogs (*Motger et al., 2026*), and single-turn user-LLM sycophancy studies except as control baselines.
